Webinar invites punch well above routine sends. Across five invites the webinar list opened at 29.5% and clicked 5.78% — well above the click rate of weekly roundups (3.90%) and more than double daily sends (2.88%). The standout is still the April 20 Steel Derivative Surtax Recovery invite: 35.8% open and a 12.37% click — 1,555 clicks from a single send, the highest-clicking mass campaign of the year. The newest invite, July 21's Duty Relief by Design webinar, opened at 27.2% and clicked 3.94% — solid but below the series average, likely because End-Use Provisions is a narrower, more technical topic than the tariff-refund sends that lead the pack. Refund- and money-themed webinar topics clearly resonate with the mass audience — the January and June surtax briefings followed the same pattern. The two breaking-news tariff blasts confirm it: urgency drives opens past 34%, while unsubscribes stayed negligible throughout (0.12% overall). July signups jumped to 69 — the strongest month since the 2025 tariff surge — while August is tracking near the ~52/month baseline so far (26 through the 17th). Keep using the mass list to fill webinar seats, and lead with concrete money-back angles where the topic allows.

Signups are holding steady at ~52/month — down from 2025's once-in-a-generation tariff surge, but in line with the normal ~59/month baseline.

The bottom line

What's working

  • Tariff urgency is the engine. The same Section 232 / IEEPA topics lead search traffic, email clicks, and webinar demand simultaneously.
  • Webinar invites are the standout email play. Five mass invites this year averaged a 5.78% click — well above the weekly roundup (3.90%) and more than double daily sends — and drove ~17% of all campaign clicks from just 5 of 149 sends. The April Steel Derivative Surtax Recovery invite hit a 12.37% click, still the year's best.
  • Targeted email outperforms — but list warmth matters within "targeted" too. Sends to specific Pipedrive client lists open at nearly double mass sends — refund-themed subject lines click best of all (W.G. McKay FTA renewal: 58% open / 30% click; a May CARM/McKay send opened even higher at 46.8%). July's Duty Relief webinar invite (14,753 sent combined) confirms the pattern at scale: the warm client list opened at 28.0% vs. 19.5% for the colder prospect list.
  • Canada converts best. Roughly 4× the conversion rate of US visitors — Canada-focused campaigns return the most per effort.
  • Webinars reach real buyers — repeatedly. Four invites this year, 1,313 registrants from ~240–255 companies at the four reported sessions, an 83% show rate at the measured session, and an audience dominated by compliance and supply-chain decision-makers.
  • LinkedIn impressions are compounding. 165,404 impressions through Aug 15 — 2.7× the Jan–Jun total — led by the GHY125 anniversary post (56.7% engagement) and a still-climbing Amsterdam summit post (6,875 impressions, the year's largest).

What to watch

  • Broken links: audited and major redirects in place (Jun 12). The 404 page reached 11,817 views, but the audit found ~75% came from one source — the legacy /tradelines-news/ archive (8,000 old URLs) — now fixed with bulk redirect rules, along with the moved vehicle-import guides, old .html pages, the missing logo asset, and the Section 232 worksheet downloads (now pointed to the Documents page). Still open: confirming new destinations for a dozen renamed articles. Expect 404 views to fall sharply in next month's data.
  • Search titles under-deliver. The Section 232 page alone has earned 681K Google impressions but clicks through at just 1.1%. Refreshing titles on the biggest under-clicked pages could add tens of thousands of clicks a year with no new content.
  • Newsletter signups picked up in July, cooling slightly in August. July hit 69 signups — the strongest month since the 2025 tariff surge — with August tracking near the ~52/month baseline so far (26 through the 17th). Worth a signup push while tariff interest is high.
  • LinkedIn follower growth has slowed. New followers are running at ~58/month since June 10, down from ~65/month in Jan–Jun and below the 2025 pace of 70/month — even as impressions and clicks are both up sharply. Worth pairing high-performing posts with a clearer follow call-to-action.
